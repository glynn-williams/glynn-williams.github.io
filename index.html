About me

Learning design, technology and a slightly unusual route here.

I’ve spent most of my career in complex organisations where accuracy, accessibility and attention to detail matter, usually somewhere between people, technology and creative problem-solving.

01

How I got here

My career has wandered through IT infrastructure, policy, learning technology, instructional design, multimedia and creative development. Rather than seeing those as separate chapters, I think they all contribute to the same thing: understanding complicated systems and finding clearer, more human ways for people to interact with them.

I’m comfortable taking an idea from the slightly-scribbled-on-a-whiteboard stage through storyboarding, build, testing, deployment and evaluation, working closely with the people who know the subject best.

02

How I work

I like learning that is clear, purposeful and respectful of the person using it. Good interaction, storytelling and visual design can make even fairly dry subjects much easier to engage with, but none of those things should get in the way of the learning itself.

My approach is iterative: build something, test it, learn from it and improve it. Accessibility is part of that process from the beginning, not something bolted on at the end.

Talk to people, understand the problem, make something useful, and keep making it better.

03

A slightly unusual portfolio

Because much of my work has been carried out within the UK Intelligence Community, a conventional public portfolio is a little difficult. Most of the learning products I created there can’t be shared publicly because of the Official Secrets Act.

I can, however, talk about the design problems I was solving, the approaches I took, the tools I used and what I learned from the work. That’s why the Portfolio section focuses on process, outcomes and safe-to-share examples.

They’re not hidden behind a particularly clever portfolio password.

04

Away from the timeline

I studied Computing with Modern Languages at the University of Gloucestershire, which probably explains why I’m still interested in both systems and communication. I also speak French and Spanish at an intermediate level.

That mix of technical, creative and communication work has followed me throughout my career. It’s less a collection of unrelated skills and more a useful tendency to ask: how does this work, who needs to understand it, and how can I make that easier?

DESIGN · DEVELOP · DEPLOY

Case Study · Programme Design

Analyst Developer Programme (ADP)

A substantial programme designed to give all new analysts a consistent, structured foundation of knowledge and skills, developed collaboratively by the in-house team and an external training provider.

Audience
All new analysts
Tools
Articulate Storyline, Adobe Creative Suite
Delivery
Agile design, build and review cycles
Impact
More than 100 cohorts of 12–16 analysts by 2024
01

The challenge

Create a consistent learning experience for all new analysts while combining specialist subject knowledge with a coherent, accessible learning design.

02

The approach

Stakeholder consultation, learning objectives, curriculum mapping, storyboards and prototype review led into iterative agile development with regular feedback and sign-off.

03

The delivery

Modules were built with Storyline and Adobe Creative Suite, quality-checked for accessibility and browser/device behaviour, then packaged to SCORM for phased LMS/LXP deployment.

04

The outcome

By 2024, more than 100 cohorts of 12–16 analysts had completed the programme. Early alignment and feedback helped improve consistency, sequencing and delivery across the learning experience.

What I took from it

The project reinforced the value of close collaboration, early agreement on learning outcomes and standards, and ongoing evaluation. It also strengthened my ability to coordinate work across organisational boundaries while keeping learner needs at the centre.
